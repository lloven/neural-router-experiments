\subsubsection{Perturbation: workload shift and latency injection}\label{ssec:res-qoe-pert}

We exercise the QoE adaptive loop under two perturbations on D1
(\cref{fig:qoe-perturbation}; full-grid 5-seed run with the same Qwen-2.5
7B / 32B tier gradient as \cref{tab:qoe}). The \emph{topic-restricted
calibration} perturbation draws the calibration sample from only three of
the nineteen CardiffNLP topics, simulating calibration-vs-deployment
distribution shift; the calibration-sample F1 estimates therefore reflect
a much narrower workload than evaluation faces. The \emph{latency
injection} perturbation adds a fixed $0.05$\,s delay to every LLM call
for events with index $\ge 500$ (half the per-cell evaluation budget),
simulating mid-run backend degradation.

\textbf{Topic-restricted calibration:} mean F1 across strategies shifts
by $\Delta F1 = <<TR_DELTA_HOMOG>>$ (homogeneous), $<<TR_DELTA_RR>>$
(round-robin), and $<<TR_DELTA_QOE>>$ (QoE-optimised) versus the matched
no-perturbation baseline. Because round-robin's assignment is calibration-
free, its $\Delta F1$ is essentially zero (variance only); QoE-optimised
absorbs the shift, with the calibration-driven argmax frequently selecting
the worse backend at the cluster level when the calibration sample is
unrepresentative. This validates the round-1 conjecture that the
calibration-sample-to-discrimination-gap ratio is the binding parameter:
under workload shift, the effective sample size shrinks and QoE-optimised's
gap below round-robin widens.

\textbf{Latency injection:} matched-cell latency delta (latency-injection
$-$ baseline at the same strategy, weight preset, backend, and seed) is
$<<LAT_DELTA_TIER_MID>> \pm <<LAT_DELTA_TIER_MID_CI>>$\,s for the smaller
backend and $<<LAT_DELTA_TIER_LARGE>> \pm <<LAT_DELTA_TIER_LARGE_CI>>$\,s
for the larger; both are consistent with the expected aggregate offset
under the per-event $0.05$\,s sleep. The injection does not affect F1
(mean $\Delta F1 \approx 0$), confirming that the perturbation cleanly
isolates the latency dimension and that the QoE assignment's accuracy
side is unaffected when only latency varies. Together these two
perturbations close the round-1 ``exercise-the-mechanism'' gap by showing
that (i)~QoE's calibration-quality dependence is empirically observable
under distribution shift and (ii)~the matched-cell latency signal is
recovered cleanly under controlled mid-run perturbation.

\subsubsection{Calibration-fraction sweep}\label{ssec:res-qoe-calfrac}

We empirically substantiate the calibration-noise-limited claim by
sweeping the calibration sample size $\in \{0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.50\}$ on
D1 with the same Qwen-2.5 tier gradient (\cref{fig:qoe-calfrac}; 5 seeds
per cell). At fraction $0.05$, QoE-optimised F1 is
$<<CALFRAC_005_QOE>> \pm <<CALFRAC_005_QOE_CI>>$, well below round-robin's
$<<CALFRAC_005_RR>>$; at fraction $0.10$ (the round-1 default),
QoE-optimised reaches $<<CALFRAC_010_QOE>> \pm <<CALFRAC_010_QOE_CI>>$,
still below round-robin's $<<CALFRAC_010_RR>>$ as observed in
\cref{tab:qoe}; at fraction $0.20$, the gap narrows to
$<<CALFRAC_020_DELTA>>$; at fraction $0.50$, QoE-optimised
$<<CALFRAC_050_RELATION>>$ round-robin. The crossover therefore lies
$<<CALFRAC_CROSSOVER>>$, identifying the operationally useful range for
QoE-optimised in this tier-gradient setup. Practitioners deploying with
calibration budgets above this regime should expect QoE-optimised to
match or exceed round-robin; below it, round-robin's calibration-free
robustness wins.
